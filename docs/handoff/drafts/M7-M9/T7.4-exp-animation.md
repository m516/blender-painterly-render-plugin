- SPEC §3: with `chain = row` (the Blender default) every (row, pass) starts at a hashed K0, and a pixel's sequence starts

- 006, Conclusion point 1: "With a fixed camera, image-plane locking and scene locking are the same thing. This experiment

### Hypothesis

#### Common definitions
- **Scene and clips.** `build/scenes/smallpaint_reference.blend` (T5.4): the SPEC §9 scene with Blender world coordinates
  equal to smallpaint coordinates, a perspective camera at the origin (18 mm lens, 36 mm sensor, sensor fit AUTO, so
  `tan(FOV/2) = 36/(2·18) = 1`), point-light ghosting only. Two clips are generated from it (`make_animation.py`), keyed at
  every frame 1…17, frame 1 being the unchanged reference camera:
  - `truck`: the camera translates along world +y (its +X, image right; T5.4) by `TRUCK_STEP = 0.055` per frame. Derivation:
    the motion per frame is `MOTION_FRACTION = 1/200` of the image side, 006's lag unit; on the image plane at unit distance
    that is `2·tan(FOV/2)/200 = 0.01`; the back wall (SPEC §9 id 4, z = −5.5) is fronto-parallel, so a translation of
    `0.01 · 5.5` moves its image left by exactly `τ(s) = s/200` px (2 px at 400 px). The camera's rotation is constant,
    so `F_camera` is constant (SPEC §1).
  - `pan`: the camera yaws to the right about its own vertical axis by `PAN_STEP = atan(0.01) = 0.0099997` rad (0.573°) per
    frame, so the image centre moves left by τ px per frame. `F_camera` rotates with the camera; `F_world` does not.
  - 1/200 of the side per frame is slow: a full side in 200 frames (8.3 s at Blender's default 24 fps). Over 16 frames the
    truck moves the back wall by 32 px at 400 px (8% of the side) and the pan turns 9.17°. All five static planes stay
    visible.
  - Rendered frames: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (four **steps** 1→2 … 4→5) and 17 (the **drift** 1→17).
- **Configurations** (six; spiral frames are compared only on the pan, because under a pure translation `F_camera` is
  constant and equals `F_world = I` up to the float32 rounding of the reference camera's rotation, which design check 5
  reports):

  | name | clip | `seed_per_frame` | `spiral_frame` |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `truck_off` | truck | false | camera |
  | `truck_on` | truck | true | camera |
  | `pan_off_camera` | pan | false | camera |
  | `pan_on_camera` | pan | true | camera |
  | `pan_off_world` | pan | false | world |
  | `pan_on_world` | pan | true | world |

- **Sequences and pairs.** A sequence is one base seed rendered at the six frames through Blender at 400 × 400 px,
  P = 64 passes per frame (006), `chain = row` and every other socket at its default. Sequence k (0…15) of configuration c
  (0…5, table order) has base seed `(16·c + k) · 18`; with `seed_per_frame` the engine uses `base + frame` (T4.3). The stride
  18 = 17 + 1 (the last frame + 1) keeps the engine-seed sets of the 96 sequences pairwise disjoint: a sequence's set is
  its base seed alone with `seed_per_frame` off, and `base + f` for each rendered frame f with it on. Pair i is sequences
  (2i, 2i+1): two independent replicates. 8 pairs per configuration.
- **Fields** (units: SPEC §7 display bytes, 0…255). `sum = pixels · 255 · P` from the scene-linear Render Result (exposure 1;
  the inverse of T4.3's `film.scene_linear`, as T5.4 does; it differs from the core's sums by the float32 rounding of
  `pixels`). `Y = luminance(smallpaint_display(sum, P))`. `h = gaussian_highpass(Y, σ(s))` with `σ(s) = (9/√12)·s/400` px
  (006). For a pair, `Y_ab = luminance(smallpaint_display(sum_a + sum_b, 2P))` (as `metrics.measure`'s `ab8`).
- **Regions**: the static planes 3-7 (floor, back wall, left, right, ceiling; SPEC §9; the front plane 8 is behind the camera).
  Spheres are excluded: mirror and glass are view-dependent by construction, and the diffuse sphere is a UV-sphere mesh whose
  surface is not the analytic sphere the reprojection would intersect (T5.4). `E_r(t)`: the pixels of frame t whose object id
  (the core's object-id pass, mapped to SPEC §9 ids) is r, eroded by `e(s) = 4·s/400` px (006). A region whose V or I mask
  (below) is empty in any step of any clip is excluded everywhere and reported.
- **Reprojection** of a step (t → t′) for region r. For each pixel p = (row, col) of frame t′, the ray through its centre
  (raster `(col + 0.5, row + 0.5)`, T5.1) meets plane r at the world point X(p). `q(p)` is the position of X(p) in frame t, as
  fractional pixel indices. `V_r(t→t′)`: the p in `E_r(t′)` whose four bilinear neighbours of q(p) lie inside the image and in
  `E_r(t)`. `I_r(t, t′) = E_r(t′) ∩ E_r(t)` (the same screen position). `w(f)` is f bilinearly sampled at q(p). Erosion is
  `metrics.erode`; the cameras are the evaluated `projection` and `camera_to_world` recorded per frame.
- **Statistics of one step** for a pair (a, b). `cov_M` is the population covariance over mask M, each side centred by its
  own masked mean (006).
  - `ρ_scene = ½[cov_V(h_a′, w h_b) + cov_V(h_b′, w h_a)] / sqrt(cov_V(h_a′, h_b′) · cov_V(w h_a, w h_b))`, where primes mark
    frame t′. a and b are independent replicates, so each cross-covariance measures only the structure they share: the
    converged texture. ρ_scene is that texture's correlation between the frames at the **same surface points**
    (dimensionless). It is the frame-pair analogue of 006's `slag`, with the lag replaced by the camera motion.
  - `ρ_image`: the same over `I` with no warp: the converged texture's correlation at the **same screen position**.
  - `ν_scene = cov_V(n′, w n) / sqrt(var_V(n′) · var_V(w n))` with `n = h_a − h_b`, and `ν_image` likewise over I without
    warp. The structure cancels in n, so ν is the correlation of the seed-dependent noise between consecutive frames of the
    same two sequences (dimensionless).
  - `Φ` (flicker, display bytes): `½ Σ_{k∈{a,b}} sqrt(mean_V (Y_k′ − w Y_k)²)`, the RMS change of one sequence's luminance at
    the same surface points.
- **Per-pair keys** (r ∈ measured regions): `r{r}.rho_scene`, `r{r}.rho_image`, `r{r}.nu_scene`, `r{r}.nu_image` and
  `r{r}.flicker`, each the mean over the four steps; `r{r}.drift = |mean_V (Y_ab,17 − w Y_ab,1)|` over `V_r(1→17)` (display
  bytes: the change of the region's brightness at the same surface points over the clip); `r{r}.rho_drift`, ρ_scene of
  1→17. Frame-1 floors: `r{r}.slag_x = noise_corrected_lag(h_a, h_b, E_r(1), axis=1, lag=τ)` (the texture's own
  autocorrelation at the step's displacement), `r{r}.structure_std` (006, on `E_r(1)`), and
  `r{r}.flicker_hold_on = sqrt(mean_{E_r(1)} (Y_a − Y_b)²)`: what a held camera shows with `seed_per_frame` on (two
  independent seeds, same camera). With `seed_per_frame` off a held camera repeats its frame exactly (design check 3).
- **Significance.** Family-wise level `SIGNIFICANCE = 0.01` (the convention of every experiment, `ensemble.py`). Exact
  two-sample permutation p-values (`ensemble.permutation_pvalue`; C(16, 8) = 12870 splits, so every test is exact) and exact
  sign-flip p-values (`ensemble.sign_flip_pvalue`, n = 8 ≤ 20), Holm (`ensemble.holm`). Power: two-sample one-sided minimum
  p 1/12870 = 7.77e-5, two-sided 1.55e-4; sign-flip one-sided 1/256 = 3.91e-3. Each family's first Holm threshold is stated
  with it below, and each is at least its minimum p.

#### H1 — Under a camera translation, the converged texture stays on the surfaces (mechanism)
- Derived from: 003 (the texture depends on the chain's prefix over a finite window, which saturates by L = 64-128 px) and
  006 point 1. If the texture is a function of the geometry along the chain within that window, a translation that shifts the
  geometry by τ px shifts the texture with it: **scene-locked**, `ρ_scene ≈ 1` and `ρ_image ≈ slag_x(τ)`. If it is a
  function of screen position (the hashed K0 of each row, the row start at column 0, the jitter hashed by (row, col, pass)),
  it stays on the screen: **image-locked**, `ρ_image ≈ 1` and `ρ_scene ≈ slag_x(τ)`.
- Separating contrast: the truck's back wall (r4), where q(p) − p is the exact integer shift (0, τ), so neither reading is
  helped by interpolation. Per pair, `d = r4.rho_image − r4.rho_scene`.
- Test: for each of `truck_off` and `truck_on`, the one-sided sign-flip p-values of "mean d > 0" (image-locked) and
  "mean d < 0" (scene-locked). Holm across the two configurations, separately for each direction, at 0.01 (first threshold
  0.005 ≥ 3.91e-3).
- Reading: **scene-locked** iff "mean d < 0" is Holm-rejected in both; **image-locked** iff "mean d > 0" is rejected in
  both; **mixed** otherwise.
- Decision: H1 supported iff scene-locked; refuted iff image-locked; inconclusive iff mixed.
- Estimation (no verdict): per configuration, mean ± sd of `r4.rho_scene`, `r4.rho_image`, `r4.slag_x` and d; the same keys
  for the other regions of the truck (bilinear reprojection) and for the pan. For each of those regions, and for the pan,
  the report states whether the sign of its mean d equals r4's and lists the regions where it does not; no verdict is read
  from it (H1's rule above is unchanged).

#### H2 — `seed_per_frame` acts only on the seed-dependent part (mechanism)
- **H2a (negative control).** Derived from the estimator: a and b are independent whichever seed each frame gets, so the
  cross-replicate covariances estimate the covariance of the per-frame expectations over seeds, which do not depend on
  `seed_per_frame`. Test: `report.family_test(off, on, keys, 0.01/3)` for each of the three seed contrasts (`truck_off` vs
  `truck_on`, `pan_off_camera` vs `pan_on_camera`, `pan_off_world` vs `pan_on_world`), keys `r{r}.rho_scene` and
  `r{r}.rho_image` of the measured regions (10; first Holm threshold 3.33e-4 ≥ 1.55e-4). Supported iff no key is rejected in
  any contrast. A rejection means the estimator or the independence of the replicates is broken; the Conclusion must say so
  before any decision is read.
- **H2b (where the seed noise goes).** Derived from SPEC §3: with `seed_per_frame` off, every frame repeats each row's K0 and
  each pixel's jitter, but the K sequence of pixel p starts after the draws consumed by the pixels before it in its row, and a
  camera move changes that content. Prediction: the noise is **re-randomized** by the move in both policies, so neither ν
  differs between them. The alternatives are **screen-held** noise (a shower-door pattern: `ν_image(off) > ν_image(on)`) and
  **surface-held** noise (`ν_scene(off) > ν_scene(on)`). Test: for each seed contrast and each of `nu_image` and `nu_scene`,
  the one-sided permutation p-values of "off greater than on" over the measured regions, Holm at 0.01/6 within each of the six
  families (first threshold 3.33e-4 ≥ 7.77e-5). Readings: screen-held iff `nu_image` rejects at least one region in each of the
  three contrasts; surface-held likewise for `nu_scene`; re-randomized iff no family rejects any region. H2b supported iff
  re-randomized; refuted iff screen-held or surface-held (name which); inconclusive otherwise.

#### H3 — Under a pan, a spiral frame fixed in the world is more stable than the camera frame (decision D2)
- Derived from SPEC §1 and §4 and 002 H1. A pan rotates `F_camera` by the camera's rotation, which changes the bounce
  distribution `d = N + F·s` of every static surface, and with it the light that reaches it through the unnormalized bounce
  and the ghost spheres. `F_world` does not change. In this scene `F_world = I` equals `F_camera` at frame 1 up to float32
  rounding (`R_cam→world · M_s→cam = I`, T5.4), so `world` here is "the camera frame of frame 1, frozen".
- Cells (30): contrasts `pan_off_world` vs `pan_off_camera` and `pan_on_world` vs `pan_on_camera`, × the measured regions,
  × keys `rho_scene` (higher is better), `flicker` and `drift` (lower is better).
- Decision: rule R below with D = `camera`, X = `world`. H3 supported iff R selects `world`. Refuted iff `world` is
  Holm-worse in at least one cell and Holm-better in none. Inconclusive otherwise.

#### H4 — `seed_per_frame = true` brings no scene-referred gain under camera motion (decision D1)
- Derived from H2b's prediction: if the move re-randomizes the seed noise in both policies, flicker and drift at the same
  surface points are equal in expectation.
- Cells (30): the three seed contrasts (X = on, D = off) × the measured regions × keys `flicker` and `drift` (lower is
  better). `rho_scene` is excluded: it is H2a's negative control and cannot depend on the policy in expectation.
- Decision: rule R with D = `false`, X = `true`. H4 supported iff `true` is Holm-better in no cell; refuted iff R selects
  `true`; inconclusive otherwise.

#### Rule R (D → X): when a candidate replaces the default
For a cell set C, with 8 vs 8 pairs from disjoint seeds:
1. Per cell, `effect = ensemble.effect_summary(X runs, D runs, [key])[key]`. The signed relative improvement is
   `g = rel_diff` for a higher-is-better key and `g = −rel_diff` for a lower-is-better key (`rel_diff = (m_X − m_D)/|m_D|`).
   A cell with `m_D = 0` has no g; it is reported and left out of step 3.
2. Per cell, `report.directional_pair_test(X runs, D runs, key, 0.01)`: `p_better` is `p_greater` for a higher-is-better key
   and `p_less` otherwise; `p_worse` is the other one. Holm (`ensemble.holm`) across all cells of C at 0.01, separately for
   the "better" and the "worse" p-values (first threshold 0.01/30 = 3.33e-4 ≥ 7.77e-5).
3. `Δ` = the median of g over the cells (006 H3's statistic). Its 99% percentile bootstrap interval: pairs are resampled
   with replacement within each configuration, `BOOTSTRAP_RESAMPLES = 10_000`, PRNG `jax.random.PRNGKey(0)` folded with the
   configuration's index. The resample count sets only the Monte Carlo precision of the interval.
4. **X replaces D iff** (a) the interval lies entirely above 0, (b) at least one cell is Holm-better, and (c) no cell is
   Holm-worse. Otherwise D stays.
- Why no threshold is needed: 0.01 is the family-wise level of the Holm tests of (b) and (c). Step (a)'s 99% percentile
  interval has nominal 99% coverage of the median of g under the resampling scheme, which is not a family-wise test
  level. No minimum effect size is set. (a) requires the typical cell to improve beyond the replicate noise, which the
  interval carries (the noise floor); (b) and (c) pair it with complete-separation tests in both directions (006
  Decision 4). A trade-off (better in some cells, worse in others) keeps the default, because this experiment defines no
  exchange rate between observables.

### Method
- **Renders.** 6 configurations × 16 sequences × 6 frames = 576 renders at 400 × 400 px, P = 64, through the installed
  extension, plus 3 check renders and 12 frame records (object ids and camera, one per clip and frame). Report the measured
  mean wall seconds per render.
- **Commands** (from the repository root):
  ```
  make env build blender extension reference-blend
  .venv/bin/python EXP/run.py --budget 540        # repeat until exit 0; exit 3 means jobs remain
  .venv/bin/python EXP/analyze.py
  ```
  Smoke (a code check only; never writes into `EXP`):
  ```
  PAINTERLY_CACHE=<scratch> .venv/bin/python EXP/run.py --smoke --budget 540   # until exit 0
  PAINTERLY_CACHE=<scratch> .venv/bin/python EXP/analyze.py --smoke --out-dir <scratch>/analysis
  ```
  `--smoke` uses 200 px (τ = 1, e = 2, σ = 1.299), P = 8 and 2 pairs (the first four sequences of each configuration).
- **Design checks** (exact; `analyze.py` raises on failure, except check 5, which is reported):
  1. `CHECK_START`: frame 1 of the `pan` clip rendered with `truck_off`'s knobs and seed equals `truck_off` sequence 0,
     frame 1 bit for bit (`np.array_equal`): both clips start at the reference camera, and identical inputs give identical
     frames through Blender.
  2. `CHECK_SEED`: frame 1 of the `truck` clip rendered with `seed_per_frame` off and seed `base + 1` equals `truck_on`
     sequence 0, frame 1 bit for bit: the engine used `seed + frame_current` (T4.3).
  3. `CHECK_HOLD`: frame 2 of the unanimated `build/scenes/smallpaint_reference.blend` (the reference camera, held) rendered
     with `truck_off`'s knobs and seed equals `truck_off` sequence 0, frame 1 bit for bit: with `seed_per_frame` off, a held
     camera repeats its frame exactly, whatever the frame number.
  4. The frame-1 camera records of both clips are identical (`np.array_equal` of `camera_to_world` and `projection`), and the
     truck's 3 × 3 rotation is identical at every rendered frame (so `F_camera` is constant on the truck).
  5. Reported, not asserted: `max |R_cam→world(1) · M_s→cam − I|` (the float32 rounding of the camera's Euler rotation; T3.13
     notes `cosf(float(π/2)) = −4.37e-8`), and `max |q(p) − p − (0, τ)|` over the truck's back-wall pixels of every step.
  6. The two sequences of every pair differ at every frame (not `np.array_equal`), and the engine-seed sets of the 96
     sequences are pairwise disjoint, a sequence's set being its base seed with `seed_per_frame` off and `base + f` for
     each rendered frame f with it on: the replicates are independent. Drift guard against the frozen prose, whose numbers
     are `PRE_REGISTERED_SEQUENCES = 16` and `PRE_REGISTERED_SEED_STRIDE = 18` (both citing "Sequences and pairs" above):
     for every configuration index c in 0…5 and sequence k in 0…15, `design.base_seed(c, k)` equals
     `(PRE_REGISTERED_SEQUENCES * c + k) * PRE_REGISTERED_SEED_STRIDE`, so a change to `PAIRS` or `FRAME_END` fails here
     rather than leaving the README's `(16·c + k) · 18` stale.
  7. Every measured region has non-empty V and I masks in every step and in the drift of both clips (else it is excluded
     everywhere and reported).
  8. Every pixel of each figure crop (below) has object id 4 in the frame it shows.
